# The editorial pass — 21 Aug 2026

A full outside read of the built book came back, and it is the most useful
critique the project has had. This document is its operational form: what is
protected, what gets cut, and the three marks that govern every future edit.
The full text of the critique is in the session record; this is what survives
of it as instruction.

## The verdict in one line

> The book is best when it behaves like a field guide written by someone who
> accidentally became a philosopher, rather than a philosopher trying to teach
> the reader how to pay attention.

## Protected — do not improve these

- **The preface, exactly as written.** "Keep. Do not make this grander."
- **The opening of *Most of Life Is a Tuesday*** — the dog and the afternoon
  sun. It "earns the entire essay."
- **The Lake Harriet field note** — "may actually contain the entire philosophy
  of the book in miniature."
- **"The pot will boil while the dog wakes."** and the final movement into
  "while we are here, we answer when we can."
- **"You have never once experienced a room. You have experienced a
  paraphrase."** — deserves its visual pause.
- **"held together from beside"** · **"The room is not a backdrop for thought."**
  · **"Time has not been removed from the journey. It has been made into one of
  its materials."** · **"Absence was not a setting selected from a menu."** ·
  **"Explanation is not the end of wonder"** — marked KEEP EXACTLY.
- **The three reproduced Meta records.** Three reads as intentional and
  mysterious; six would read as a design device. **No fourth dataset.**
- **The prairie dog** doing absolutely nothing, where it is — the tonal relief
  after the mortality material is load-bearing.

## The three marks

Every prose edit from now on uses exactly these:

- **KEEP** — sentences where the book sounds unmistakably like itself.
- **TRUST THE READER** — the point has been made; the restatement goes. The
  white space, the images, the specimens are allowed to finish the sentence.
- **MAKE IT PHYSICAL** — an abstraction becomes a room, object, animal,
  measurement, gesture, or observed event — or it goes. The test sentence for
  Part III: *"Can I see this happening instead of being told about it?"*

Expected total effect: 8–12% of the prose comes out, and the book reads larger.

## The cut list, in order

1. **Essay 1, final third** — the thesis is restated through dog, coffee,
   walking, seasons, love, illness, travel and routine, and then again in
   the mountain/summit metaphor. "We already believe you." Cut 10–15% of the
   explanatory prose. DONE 21 Aug — see the diff.
2. **Part III, *The Strange Privilege*** — keep the technological scope, lose
   the white-paper register. Transistor counts, AlphaFold, labour-role shifts
   read like a technology publication. The model passage is the human revising
   the machine's paragraph — a person in a room making decisions. DONE 21 Aug
   for the two worst passages; the register rule stands for future edits.
3. **The back cover** — "the people who will not always be here" overweights
   mortality against the actual book, which contains much more life than
   death. Reframed around shared time. DONE 21 Aug.
4. **The closing mycelium illustration** — possibly too botanically familiar
   for a book that has developed its own visual vocabulary. UNDER REVIEW —
   removing an image from a composed spread is a layout decision, recorded in
   open-questions for Adam.

## Guardrails the critique set

- **No more mortality material.** The final 15 pages sit exactly at the line
  between "look how alive everything is" and "pay attention because you're
  going to die." The book is on the right side. Nothing tips it.
- **Watch the aphoristic register.** "Attention is the only genuinely
  nonrenewable thing you have" is at the edge of self-help language; the prose
  after it saves it. It stays, and nothing else gets written in that register.
- **Part II is the book's distinctiveness** — the hidden-systems material is
  what keeps this from being another mindfulness book. If anything it may get
  *stranger* visually, never tamer.

## The visual hierarchy, named

The design languages are many and independently good; what was missing was a
stated order of authority, so the outliers read as intentional:

1. **Primary: documentary observation** — the photographs.
2. **Secondary: analytical and survey overlay** — the drafted diagrams.
3. **Tertiary: found record, archive, specimen** — the Meta records, the
   catalogue cards, the handwriting.

Recorded in [art-direction.md](art-direction.md) as a standing rule.
