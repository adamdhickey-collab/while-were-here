# Photo selection 03 — the trips folder

182 stills across two trips: Maui, April 2015, and Washington DC, May 2016.
Source: `~/Desktop/trips`. No duplicates.

**18 selected. 1 declined on ethical grounds. About 20 blocked on rights.**

Candid rule applies throughout. Numbers in the captions below are registered in
[facts.json](../facts.json) with sources.

---

## What this folder is for

Neither of the earlier folders could serve **Part III**. The travel folder gave
Part IV its pilgrimage imagery and the dated folder gave Part I its lake and its
domestic frames, but *The Strange Privilege of Being Alive During a
Technological Revolution* and *The Last Generation That Remembers the
Before-Time* had almost nothing.

This one solves both, from one afternoon in a museum.

It also turns out to hold the best single candid frame in all three folders, and
it is not in a museum at all.

## Selected

### The frame the whole book is arguing for

**T104 · P1050253** · a grackle standing on a bin lid among plastic cups, three
people eating a metre away, none of them looking at it
Role PLATE · Attention, image-essay recto · Stage I · 4000 × 3000
- **Crop** Keep the people. They are soft and half-cropped and that is the
  point. Cutting to the bird makes it a wildlife photograph and destroys the
  argument.
- **Color** As shot.
- **Texture** Global paper only.
- **Caption** `Quiscalus quiscula, working a lunch queue. Nobody at the table
  turned around.`
- **Why** A wild animal operating fluently inside a human system while the humans
  remain unaware of it. That is the book's thesis in one frame, made without a
  word of help. It is the strongest candid photograph in any of the three
  folders.
- **Note** `T105` is the same moment a second later, with a raised hand. Keep it
  as the alternate; the pair could also run as a sequence.

**T101 · P1050250** · a single sparrow on a wide field of pale gravel
Role SPECIMEN · Attention or Ordinary Days, margin · Stage I · 4000 × 3000
- **Crop** Do not centre it. Keep the gravel dominant and the bird small.
- **Color** As shot. The palette is already bone and grey.
- **Caption** `One sparrow, and about four thousand stones.`
- **Why** Maximum negative space, minimum event. Pairs naturally with the
  cat-drinking frame from folder one.

**T111 · P1050261** · water spreading across granite paving in a blot, someone's
feet at the top edge
Role MAP or TEXTURE · Systems, ground under type at low opacity · Stage II ·
4000 × 3000
- **Crop** Square. Keep the feet: they give scale and stop it being abstract.
- **Color** Drop to 12 to 18 percent as a ground, per the MAP rule, or run it
  full. Never in between.
- **Caption** `Nobody drew this and it will be gone in an hour.`
- **Why** Accidental cartography. A coastline that made itself, on a pavement,
  with a person standing in it.

### Part III · machines, and the surfaces they came back with

**T023 · IMG_1789** · Apollo 11 Command Module *Columbia*, close on the scorched
hull, rivets, windows and hatch fittings
Role MATERIAL · **III → IV stage turn**, full-bleed · 3264 × 2448
- **Crop** Fill the frame with the surface. No museum floor, no rope, no label.
- **Color** As shot. The scorch pattern is the subject and any correction kills it.
- **Texture** None. This surface has enough of its own.
- **Caption** `Columbia's shield worked by destroying itself. The ablative layer
  vaporised as it heated and carried the heat away with it, off a surface running
  between roughly 1,500 and 5,000 °F.` Sourced: `apollo-heat-shield`.
- **Why** A material break wants "a surface that is not paper, photographed at
  macro scale so it reads as terrain before it reads as substance." This is
  that, and it is also an object whose whole method was controlled
  self-destruction, landing on the turn into Stage IV. `T022` is the wider view
  and is the reserve.

**T033 · IMG_1800** · full-scale model of the Block Island meteorite, in black
and white, its own museum label in frame
Role PLATE · The Intelligence Outside Your Head, or Technological Revolution ·
Stage II or III · 3264 × 2448
- **Crop** Keep the label. It is doing the specimen-card job already.
- **Color** It arrived monochrome. Leave it.
- **Caption** `Block Island. Iron and nickel, about 67 cm across, found on Mars
  by the Opportunity rover on 17 July 2009 and still there. This is a copy.`
  Sourced: `block-island-meteorite`.
- **Why** A museum object that is a replica of a rock no human has touched,
  found by a robot that is no longer working, on another planet. For an essay
  about intelligence outside the head this is almost too apt.

**T059 · IMG_4261** · a mineral case: amethyst, quartz, malachite and coral on
white plinths, each with a printed label
Role PLATE · Systems, or a divider · Stage II · 2448 × 3264
- **Crop** Keep several plinths and their labels. The repetition is the point.
- **Color** As shot.
- **Caption** Short. The labels in frame do the work.
- **Why** The book invents a specimen-card language. Here is that language
  already existing, photographed in the wild, which quietly legitimises the
  whole apparatus. `T060` and `T057` are the alternates.

**T065 · IMG_4277** · four blank segments of the Berlin Wall behind a blue rope
Role PLATE · The Last Generation That Remembers the Before-Time · Stage III ·
3264 × 2448
- **Crop** Wide. Keep the rope and the floor.
- **Color** As shot. Grey concrete on a dark stage.
- **Caption** `The back of it. This side faced the people who were kept in.`
  **Verify before setting** which face is which in this installation.
- **Why** The before-time as an object with a rope round it. Blank concrete says
  more here than the painted side does, and the rope says the most of all.
- **Note** `T066` is the graffitied face and is more graphic, but the paintwork
  is authored by identifiable artists, so it carries a rights question the blank
  side does not.

## Declined

**T095 and T096 · the Tower of Faces, United States Holocaust Memorial Museum.**
Two frames of the shaft of family photographs from Eišiškės.

These are the most powerful images in the folder and I am recommending against
using them. The book's argument about ordinary days and temporary people is
genuinely adjacent to what that room is, and that adjacency is exactly the
problem. Those photographs are evidence of a specific genocide and of one town
whose Jewish community was murdered in two days in September 1941. Lifting the
room into a general meditation on mortality, in a book that does not otherwise
engage with the Holocaust at all, would be borrowing that weight rather than
earning it.

If the book ever does address it directly, with the town named and the history
stated, that changes. As an atmospheric note about how everyone is temporary, no.

Your call and not mine, but I would want the reason on the record either way.

## Blocked on rights

About twenty frames, in three groups.

**The Renwick "WONDER" installations (T121–T133).** Gabriel Dawe's thread
spectrum, Tara Donovan's index-card towers, Patrick Dougherty's woven sticks,
Janet Echelman's net. All by living artists and all instantly identifiable.
Donovan's towers are painful to lose, because thousands of identical index cards
accumulating into something that reads as geology is *The Beauty of Systems
Nobody Designed* almost verbatim. It was designed, though, and by someone with a
name and a gallery.

**Lichtenstein's *House I* (T062) and Ruscha's *Standard Station* (T182).** Same
issue. The Lichtenstein is a real loss: it is an inverted-perspective sculpture
that refuses to look concave, which is a perception argument the attention essay
could have used.

**The National Gallery paintings (T173, T175, T176, T178).** The works
themselves are public domain, and your own photograph of a public-domain
painting is generally yours to use. But these are snapshots through glass at an
angle, and [asset-sources.md](asset-sources.md) already points at the
Smithsonian and BHL for exactly this material at plate resolution. Source the
Audubon flamingo properly rather than printing a photograph of a reproduction.
