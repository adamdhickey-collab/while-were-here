**The grackle is the best picture found in this pass.** A glossy black bird
standing on the rim of a blue bin among discarded cups, entirely sharp, with a
crowd eating at outdoor tables blurred behind it. It is the same idea as
`library-24-fatima-pigeon` and it beats it: the bird is sharp, the people are
not, and the bin is the whole argument.

**Two pairs overlap and only one of each belongs in the book.** library-42 and
library-43 are both enormous conifers photographed from below — one is a crown
radiating, the other is a bar of bark across the page. library-45 and
library-31 (the Colombian bakery) are two rooms in different hemispheres
solving the same problem by putting many identical things in order.

**One is not a full-bleed candidate.** library-51, the peppers, is 207 dpi —
comfortable for a quarter-page inset and nothing larger. Every other frame here
is 254 or better.

## Declined, with reasons

**On the candid rule** — the standing rule confirmed 19 Aug: *no group shots, no
portraits, nobody looking at the lens.*

* **P1070962** — a woman in a pale blue dress on the broad stone stair at
  Vizcaya under live oaks. Centred, facing the camera. A holiday portrait.

**On quality, at full size rather than at contact-sheet size**

* **IMG_8555** — hanging roast ducks and cured sausage in a Chinatown window.
  Handsome as a thumbnail; shot through glass and motion-blurred at print size.
* **P1070116** — a bald eagle on a bare branch with aviary mesh at the frame
  edge. The mesh does not do the work here that it does in `library-33`, the
  Colombian lion, where the fence is the thing that came out sharp.
* **photo-2373 / photo-2145** were declined in [06](photo-selection-06.md) for
  the same reason: intricate small, green noise large.

**On rights — a whole category, and worth stating once**

Miami is largely **Wynwood Walls**, and a substantial part of Chicago, DC and
Seattle is other people's work: the murals, the gallery canvases, Abakanowicz's
*Agora* in Grant Park, the painted Berlin Wall segments, Trimpin's guitar
sculpture at MoPOP. A photograph of an identified artist's work, printed full
page in a bound book, reproduces the work. The imprint already credits five
adapted photographs properly; adding a dozen murals would be a different kind of
undertaking, and none of them earns it.

**On fit — recorded so nobody rediscovers it and places it**

`P1050243` / `P1050242` — the **Tower of Faces** at the United States Holocaust
Memorial Museum, a shaft lined floor to ceiling with hundreds of family
photographs from Eišiškės, shot looking up. It is an extraordinary image and it
is, on its face, about precisely what this book is about: ordinary days,
photographed, and what survives of them.

It is not going in. A book of gentle essays on attention cannot borrow the force
of a genocide memorial as an illustration, and to use it properly would mean
writing the essay that earns it, which is not this book. **DO NOT PLACE.**
Written down because the argument for it is genuinely tempting and somebody —
including a later session of mine — will find it again.

## What this pass says about the archive as a whole

Two of the seven trips were photographed as **observations** and five as
**holiday records**. The Hoh and the Grand Canyon in December yielded five of
the eleven between them: nobody was performing, the weather was doing the work,
and the camera was pointed at things rather than at people in front of things.
New York, Miami and Chicago yielded three between them, all of them frames where
Adam happened to be looking at a bin, a crate or a crossing rather than at a
landmark.

That is not a criticism of the trips. It is the reason this book was always
going to be built mostly from the ordinary days rather than the exceptional
ones, and the archive says so more plainly than the essays do.
