# Photo selection 08 — the home archive

**15,731 frames reviewed**, every Minneapolis frame in the library, 2015 to 2026,
ranked year by year so no single day could fill the sheet. Source:
`~/Desktop/photo libraries/photo library 2`.

**6 selected.** Registered as `library-52` through `library-57`.

That is the lowest yield of any pass in this project — 0.04% against Colombia's
0.5% — and the reason is not that the pictures are bad. It is that the home
archive is a **garden, a dog and a family**, photographed lovingly and at length,
and this book already has its personal photographs. What it still wants from
home is on the [shot list](shot-list.md), and almost none of it has been taken.

**library-52 and library-53 are a pair and should be placed together or not at
all.** The same grey exposed-aggregate pavement, both shot straight down, both
holding exactly one thing and nothing else — a leaf that came down heart-shaped,
and a caterpillar crossing. Taken two years and one month apart, neither planned.
As a spread they make the essay's argument twice without a word: the same
ground, looked at twice, holding something different each time.

**library-52 is the best single frame in the entire home archive.** A pavement
anybody would walk over, photographed by somebody who stopped.

**library-57 and library-42 do the same job and only one belongs in the book.**
The Hoh conifer crown is a mass; the December oak is a diagram, because winter
took the leaves off.

## What the archive does not contain

Item 0 on the shot list — **the dog asleep in a rectangle of afternoon light** —
was already recorded as "verified absent from the library" after a search by
Photos' own scene labels. **This pass confirms it visually across all twelve
years.** It is not there. Nor is the kitchen table with a laptop, the dog under
the desk, the workbench after the work, or the evening light on a far wall.

The shot list's premise was tested against 672 frames and concluded *"nothing in
the library serves these."* It has now been tested against 15,731 and the
conclusion holds. **Those photographs have to be taken.**

## Eight of fourteen shortlisted frames were the wrong picture

This is the finding worth carrying forward, and it cost more than everything
else in this pass.

Frames were shortlisted from 30-up contact sheets and then rendered at full size
before any decision. **Eight of the fourteen turned out to be a different
photograph entirely** — what looked like a yellow warbler perched on a garden
hose was a magnolia portrait in one case and a Pride parade in another; a turtle
on a branch was a hosta; a mown path between trees was the dog on a brick walk.

The cache was not at fault: every cached thumbnail was tested against its file on
disk and matched exactly. The sheet was. `interesting.py` drew each label at the
**top-left of a fixed grid cell** while centring the image inside that cell, so a
row mixing portrait and landscape frames put every picture at a different height
and the labels drifted visually away from the pictures they named. It also
truncated the filename at 17 characters — and `IMG_7252 (2).HEIC` is exactly 17,
so anything longer lost the ` (1)` / ` (2)` suffix that is the only thing telling
four otherwise identically-named files apart.

Both are fixed: the label is now drawn **on** the thumbnail, on a white strip,
with the full filename.

**The standing rule, stated once for every future pass: a contact sheet is for
finding candidates and never for identifying them.** Nothing enters the manifest
that has not been opened at full size by its exact filename.

## Also unreviewed, and small enough to finish

**342 RAW files with no JPEG counterpart** — Pentax `.PEF`, Sony `.ARW` and
Panasonic `.RW2` frames that were shot RAW-only and so are in neither
`places.json` nor the thumbnail index. A further 507 RAWs pair with JPEGs
already reviewed and can be ignored.

Worth correcting a number reported earlier: the "8,161 frames the index has never
seen" are **not** 8,161 unseen photographs. They are 5,774 `.aae` edit sidecars,
1,468 videos, 849 RAWs and 20 `.psd` files. The genuinely unseen photographs
number 342.
