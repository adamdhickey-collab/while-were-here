# Photography prompts — QUIET SURREAL DOCUMENTARY, ALIVE

The house photographic style. Every image in the book is generated or shot against
this preamble; the per-image sections below only add subject, composition and
purpose.

**Revised 2026-08 with the final art direction** (`docs/art-direction.md` §
Photography): the photography is believable, vivid and full of life — more
alive than the earlier dark contact-sheet reading of this style. Varied
weather and seasons; clear summer greens, blue water, amber sunlight, flowers,
birds in movement, warm domestic scenes balance the quieter frames. Not every
image is dusk, gray, underexposed, melancholy, empty, brown or vignetted.
Photography should say: *look at the world.*

The emotional question behind every frame is the same:

> Why does this ordinary thing suddenly feel important?

---

## Master style preamble

Paste this ahead of any subject description.

> A believable documentary photograph of the real world. Quiet, contemplative,
> observant, naturalistic, cinematic, very slightly uncanny. Natural available
> light only — at any believable hour, not only dusk: clear mornings, full
> daylight, changing weather. Restrained composition with strong negative
> space and imperfect, slightly off-centre framing, as if the photographer did
> not have time to fix it. Believable lens behaviour: real depth of field, no
> exaggerated bokeh, vignetting only when the light itself produces it. Rich
> but credible colour — living greens, blue water, warm cream, amber light,
> true surface texture. Subtle 35 mm film grain. Emotionally present but never
> sentimental, and never gloomy.
> Shot on a 35 mm or 50 mm prime, medium format feel, shallow-to-normal depth.

## Master negative constraints

Append to every prompt.

> No fantasy. No neon, glow, bloom or hallucination effects. No floating or
> impossible objects. No glossy commercial or stock photography. No exaggerated
> HDR or heavy grading. No smiling models. No one looking into the lens unless
> the subject calls for it. No visible text, signage lettering, logos or
> watermarks. No distorted hands, faces or anatomy. No AI-render sheen. No
> symmetrical dead-centre composition. No lens flare added for effect.

## Composition rules

Each frame should carry exactly **one poetic relationship** — a rhyme the eye
finds a second after the subject. Not two. Not none.

Working examples of the relationship, not the subject:

- birds on a wire that read as handwriting
- two strangers joined only by their shadows
- one lit window among forty dark ones
- a very small person under enormous weather
- a dog following a rectangle of light across a floor
- a reflection that puts two unrelated people in the same frame
- ordinary objects that have accidentally fallen into a pattern

Use metaphor, repetition, scale, absence, juxtaposition, pattern, shadow,
reflection and unexpected cropping. Resist illustrating the sentence.

## Aspect and resolution

| Placement | Aspect | Minimum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full page | 1:1 | 4000 × 4000 px |
| Full two-page spread | 2:1 | 8000 × 4000 px |
| Vertical image + essay | 2:3 | 4000 × 6000 px |
| Bottom band / interrupt | 3:1 | 6000 × 2000 px |

Full-bleed images must carry expendable material on every trimmed edge, and a
crossover image must keep faces, horizons and any single point of interest clear
of the fold.

## Prompts in production

### attention-01-window-reflection
*Essay: The Secret Life of Attention · Spread: essay opener, verso, full page*

> [MASTER PREAMBLE] A second-floor apartment window at dusk, seen from the
> street below. It is the only lit window in a facade of many. The glass carries
> a faint reflection of bare street trees standing behind the viewer, so the room
> and the street occupy the same surface. Cool blue evening exterior against one
> square of warm interior light. The building fills the frame; sky only at the
> very top edge. Square crop. [MASTER NEGATIVES]

- **Concept.** Two worlds on one pane. You can attend to the room or to the
  reflection, never both at once.
- **Purpose.** States the essay's premise before a word of it is read.
- **Watch for.** Do not let the reflection become a double exposure. It should be
  faint enough to be missed on a first look.

### attention-03-dog-sunlight
*Essay: The Secret Life of Attention · Spread: image + essay, verso*

> [MASTER PREAMBLE] An old dog lying along the edge of a rectangle of late
> afternoon light on a worn wood floor, its body following the shape exactly.
> A rug nearby holds a faint dent where the dog lay an hour ago. Domestic
> interior, no people, no furniture in focus. Dust visible in the light. Vertical
> 2:3 frame with the dog low and a lot of empty floor above. [MASTER NEGATIVES]

- **Concept.** Sustained attention without effort. The animal is better at this
  than you are.
- **Purpose.** Carries the essay's practical claim — noticing is a matter of
  speed, not will.
- **Watch for.** The dent in the rug is the whole picture. If it is not legible,
  the frame has failed.

### attention-04-lake-weather
*Essay: The Secret Life of Attention · Spread: full bleed, crossover*

> [MASTER PREAMBLE] An enormous flat bank of weather moving over a grey
> freshwater lake, low horizon, most of the frame given to cloud. One very small
> figure stands on a concrete breakwater far to the right of centre, seen from
> behind. Cold desaturated palette — slate, faded blue, a thin band of warm light
> at the horizon. 2:1 panoramic frame. [MASTER NEGATIVES]

- **Concept.** Scale as an attention device. The eye has to travel to find the
  person.
- **Purpose.** The book's first long exhale. No text, no folio.
- **Watch for.** The figure must sit fully on the recto and the horizon must
  cross the fold cleanly.

### attention-05-crosswalk-strangers
*Essay: The Secret Life of Attention · Spread: reading, verso bottom band*

> [MASTER PREAMBLE] Four strangers waiting at a crosswalk on an overcast
> afternoon, each looking in a different direction. Their long shadows overlap on
> the asphalt into a single connected shape. Shot from across the street, slightly
> elevated, so the shadows read more clearly than the faces. Wide 3:1 band crop,
> figures small in the frame. [MASTER NEGATIVES]

- **Concept.** Four separate paraphrases of one street corner.
- **Purpose.** Interrupts the text at the foot of the verso so the spread has
  weight where it would otherwise trail off.

### part-1-divider-sidewalk-birds
*Section divider: Part I — Look Again · verso, full bleed*

> [MASTER PREAMBLE] A wet city sidewalk after rain, reflecting a pale sky.
> Above it, a scatter of starlings sitting on a power line, spaced unevenly so
> the group reads like a line of handwriting. Shot from below at a slight angle.
> Muted greys and warm concrete, one soft note of green from a hedge at the edge.
> Square crop with the wire in the upper third. [MASTER NEGATIVES]

- **Concept.** The world composing itself without intending to.
- **Purpose.** Opens Part I on a pattern that was always there.

## Personal photography

`/public/images/personal` is reserved for real photographs — family, dogs, lake,
old objects, places with meaning. Any manifest entry can be swapped to a personal
image later without touching layout code: keep the manifest ID, change `kind` to
`personal` and drop the file in. The book is designed so that personal imagery
can replace generated imagery gradually rather than all at once.
